### Demo 1: Draft a Financial Stability Report Summary (3 min)

**Prompt**: "Draft a 200-word executive summary for a Financial Stability Report covering: rising household debt (85% GDP), commercial real estate stress (18% vacancy), improving bank capital (14.2% Tier 1). Tone: measured but alert."

**Result**: Complete summary in 10 seconds. Structure, tone, caveats appropriate.

**Point**: "This is the copilot. You're still the pilot. But 2 hours → 2 minutes."

---

### Demo 2: Extract Key Policy Changes from Multiple Documents (3 min)

**Prompt**: "I'm uploading the last 3 FOMC statements. Extract and summarize: (1) changes in forward guidance language, (2) shifts in risk assessment, (3) changes in policy stance. Present in a comparison table."

**Result**: AI extracts specific language changes, identifies subtle shifts in tone, presents structured comparison showing policy evolution.

**Point**: "Document analysis that took hours of manual comparison happens in seconds. AI excels at pattern-matching across large text bodies. You still need to interpret the significance—but AI handles the extraction."

---

### Demo 3: Red-Team a Policy Decision (3 min)

**Sample text**: "To address 4.8% inflation, MPC proposes +50bp to 3.5%. Labor market tight at 3.2% unemployment. Expect return to 2-3% target within 12 months."

**Prompt**: "Review this decision. What's missing? What are the risks? What questions would you ask?"

**Result**: AI flags missing elements (exchange rate, financial stability, fiscal coordination), identifies risks (wage spiral, asset bubbles), suggests questions (output gap, neutral rate, market reaction).

**Point**: "AI is your red team. It pattern-matches against every IMF report, BIS paper, Fed decision instantly."

# PART II: WHAT WE CAN DO (30 minutes)

## 4. Historical Lessons: Why the Transition Matters More Than the Technology (8 min)

### The Pattern from Past Disruptions

**Industrial Revolution (1760-1840)**: Engels' Pause

- Technology arrived fast; wage gains took 60+ years
- Required complementary investments: education, infrastructure, legal reform

**Electricity (1880s-1920s)**: The Factory Layout Problem

- Productivity gains only appeared when factories *redesigned* around electricity
- **Lesson**: Technology ≠ Transformation

**Information Revolution (1980s-2000s)**: The Solow Paradox

- "Computers everywhere except in productivity statistics"
- Took 20+ years; meanwhile: hollowed middle class, wage stagnation, populism

### The Central Question for This Room

> **"Will we manage the AI transition better than we managed globalization? Because the social and political costs of getting this wrong are already visible."**
> 

**Key Insight**: The transitions that worked best had **strong public sector leadership** coordinating investments in education, infrastructure, legal reform, and social safety nets.

## 5. A Practical Framework: Three Pillars (12 min)

### Pillar 1: Institutional Readiness

**Governance**

- Board-level AI strategy and oversight
- Clear risk appetite and ethical guidelines
- Accountability frameworks

**Talent**

- Train economists and analysts to *work with* AI (not just hire AI engineers)
- Build AI literacy across leadership

**Infrastructure**

- Secure compute, data governance, model risk management

---

### Pillar 2: Strategic Use Cases

**Start Smart**

✅ Good starting points: Fraud/AML, forecasting with alternative data, document processing, regulatory reporting

❌ Don't start with: Core policy decisions requiring full explainability, novel regulatory frameworks without precedent

**Success Pattern**: Identify clear problem → pilot in controlled environment → measure rigorously → scale gradually → maintain human oversight

**The Cost Question: What's the Real Investment?**

Entry points vary widely based on approach:

**Low-cost start ($50K-200K annually):**
- Commercial API access (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google)
- Limited pilot use cases (2-3 teams)
- Minimal infrastructure changes
- Quick wins in document processing, research assistance

**Mid-scale deployment ($500K-2M annually):**
- Enterprise agreements with SLAs
- Custom integrations with existing systems
- Data governance and security infrastructure
- Broader organizational deployment (50-200 users)

**Strategic capability build ($5M-20M over 3-5 years):**
- Dedicated compute infrastructure
- Model fine-tuning and customization
- Comprehensive data pipelines
- Organization-wide transformation
- Singapore's approach: $1B national investment over 5 years

**ROI timeline:** Early productivity gains (20-30%) appear in months. Full workflow transformation takes 2-3 years. Most institutions see positive ROI within 12-18 months for well-scoped pilots.

**Key insight:** Start small, prove value, then scale. Don't wait for budget certainty—pilot costs are comparable to hiring one senior analyst.

- Technology evolves faster than any organization adapts alone
- Standards require coordination

**Singapore Model**: AI Singapore (research) + GovTech (deployment) + MAS (regulation) + coordinated investments in compute and talent = coherent national strategy with distributed execution.

# PART III: CHALLENGES AHEAD (25 minutes)

## 7. Five Critical Challenges for Central Banks (20 min)

### Challenge 1: Financial Stability Risks & The AI Bubble

**Carlota Perez's Framework: Why Bubbles Are Part of the Pattern**

Perez's *Technological Revolutions and Financial Capital* (2002) shows every major tech revolution follows: **bubble → crash → golden age**

**The Five Historical Revolutions:**

- Industrial Revolution (1771) → Canal/textile mania
- Railways (1829) → Railway mania, crash of 1847
- Steel/Electricity (1875) → Crash of 1890s
- Oil/Automobiles (1908) → 1929 crash and Depression
- Information Age (1971) → Dotcom bubble, 2000-2002 crash

**Perez's Two-Phase Pattern:**

*Phase 1: Installation (Financial Capital Dominates)*

- Speculation creates asset bubbles
- Infrastructure built through speculative investment
- **Inevitable crash** when reality fails to meet expectations

*Phase 2: Deployment (Production Capital Takes Over)*

- Post-crisis regulation and reform
- Technology diffuses broadly
- Productivity gains materialize
- "Golden Age" of shared prosperity—**if managed well**

**The Turning Point Requires Active Government:**

- Financial regulation to prevent systemic collapse
- Institutional reform to enable adoption
- Investment in complementary infrastructure
- Social safety nets and education transformation

**Where Are We Now?**

**Signs We're in Installation Phase:**

- $200B+ AI investment (2023-2024)
- NVIDIA market cap: $300B → $3T+ (2022-2024)
- AI startups at 50-100x revenue multiples
- $500B in data center investments announced
- "AI-washing" to boost stock prices

**Unique AI Bubble Risks:**

1. **Speed**: Railway mania took decades; AI bubble compressed to 2-3 years
2. **Concentration**: Previous revolutions distributed; AI concentrated in few providers (creates "too big to fail" dynamics)
3. **Procyclicality**: Banks using similar AI models may all act identically in crisis, amplifying boom-bust
4. **Geopolitics**: US-China competition overrides normal market discipline

**Financial Stability Implications:**

**Pre-Crash (Now):**

- Asset price inflation in AI sectors
- Misallocation of capital at scale
- Banking system exposure to AI valuations
- Pension funds and sovereign wealth overexposed

**During Crash:**

- Contagion to broader equity markets
- Credit crunch as AI-focused banks face losses
- Flight to safety, liquidity stress

**Post-Crash (The Crucial Phase):**

- Financial stabilization (traditional crisis tools)
- **But also:** Ensure crash doesn't kill productive deployment
- Distinguish speculative excess from genuine innovation
- Launch regulatory reform to enable "golden age"

**The Railway Analogy:**

Railway mania bankrupted investors but left Britain with essential infrastructure that enabled a century of growth. **Key lesson**: Bubble can be financially destructive yet economically productive.

**Central Bank Dilemma:**

- The bubble may be *necessary* to finance infrastructure build-out
- But bubbles always end badly for those caught
- Without proper management, society misses the golden age

**Policy Prescription for Central Banks:**

*Before the Crash:*

- Monitor AI asset valuations carefully
- Stress test banking exposure
- International coordination on capital flows
- **Don't try to prevent bubble** (historically impossible)
- **Do contain contagion channels**

*The Turning Point:*

- Traditional crisis response: liquidity, backstops
- **Plus**: Convene stakeholders for reform
- Protect critical AI infrastructure from fire-sale liquidation
- Distinguish genuine technology from speculation

*Enabling the Golden Age:*

- Regulatory frameworks for responsible deployment
- Public investment in complementary infrastructure
- Education and workforce transition
- Competition policy to prevent winner-take-all
- Ensure productivity gains are broadly shared

**What Central Banks Should Monitor Right Now:**

*Direct exposure metrics:*
- Banking sector holdings in AI-focused equities and bonds
- Pension fund and insurance company AI sector allocation
- Sovereign wealth fund exposure to AI infrastructure
- Credit extended to AI companies and data center developments

*Systemic risk indicators:*
- Correlation in AI model usage across major banks (procyclicality risk)
- Concentration in cloud/compute providers (single points of failure)
- Cross-border capital flows into AI investments
- Margin lending against AI stocks

*Early warning signals:*
- Divergence between AI company revenues and market valuations
- Sharp increases in retail investor participation in AI stocks
- Acceleration in data center construction without corresponding demand
- "AI-washing" in earnings calls (mentions without substance)

**Recommended stress test**: Model 50-70% correction in AI-heavy equity indices over 6-12 months. Assess: (1) direct portfolio losses, (2) credit quality deterioration, (3) contagion to broader markets, (4) liquidity stress scenarios.

### Challenge 2: The Adoption Gap

**The 95% Problem**: MIT study (2025) found 95% of GenAI pilots fail to deliver value

**Why?** Technology exists, but organizations lack:

- Change management and cultural readiness
- Data infrastructure and quality
- Skills to deploy and maintain
- Clear ROI metrics

**Central banks especially vulnerable**: Legacy systems, risk-averse culture, long procurement cycles, strict compliance, limited room to experiment.

---

### Challenge 3: Labor Market Disruption

**This Time Is Different**: AI automates *non-routine* cognitive work (research, analysis, even some policy-making)

**Key Distinctions:**

- AI optimizes; humans prioritize (AI finds efficient routes; you decide if destination is worth it)
- AI recognizes patterns; humans break them (innovation requires questioning status quo)
- AI processes data; humans build trust (relationships and legitimacy remain human)
- AI follows instructions; humans write the rules (ethical judgment stays with people)

**Institutional Implications**: Junior analysts most at risk; mid-career needs reskilling; senior judgment more valuable than ever.

---

### Challenge 4: Geopolitics & Sovereignty

**Recent Developments:**

- US export controls on AI chips (H100, H200)
- Concentration of frontier AI in US + China
- Tier-2 countries face compute quotas

**Implications**: Dependence on foreign AI infrastructure, data sovereignty concerns, need for regional capabilities

**Strategic Options for Smaller Economies:**

**Don't try to compete on foundation models** (requires billions in capital, specialized talent, massive compute)

**Instead, focus on:**

1. **Application Layer Excellence**
   - Build AI solutions for local problems (regulatory compliance, regional languages, domestic market needs)
   - Use APIs from frontier models as infrastructure
   - Develop domain expertise, not compute capacity

2. **Regional Collaboration**
   - Pool resources with neighboring countries (SEA-LION model: 11 countries, shared investment)
   - Share compute infrastructure through regional data centers
   - Coordinate on standards and governance frameworks

3. **Open-Source Leverage**
   - Deploy and fine-tune open models (Llama, Mistral, regional models)
   - Contribute improvements back to community
   - Reduce vendor lock-in and dependency risks

4. **Strategic Partnerships**
   - Negotiate sovereign cloud agreements with major providers
   - Require data residency and local processing
   - Build "AI-ready" infrastructure even if not building models

**SEA-LION Case Study**: Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand, Philippines, Vietnam, Cambodia, Laos, Myanmar, Brunei, and Timor-Leste pooled resources to build regional LLM capability. No single country could have done it alone. Result: reduced dependence, improved local language support, shared costs.

**Key Insight**: Sovereignty doesn't mean autarky. Strategic interdependence through regional collaboration beats dependency on distant superpowers.

---

### Challenge 5: Trust & Explainability

**Why It Matters for Central Banks:**

- Require public trust and legitimacy
- Decisions affect millions
- "Black box" models undermine accountability

**The Explainability Trilemma**: Can typically achieve only 2 of 3:

- Accuracy (model performs well)
- Interpretability (humans understand how)
- Complexity (handles nuanced real-world problems)

**Emerging Standards**: Model cards, explainable AI techniques (SHAP, LIME), human-in-the-loop validation, audit trails.

## Three Imperatives for This Room

### 1. Invest in Adoption Architecture, Not Just Technology

The "boring" infrastructure matters most:

- Education and reskilling
- Legal and regulatory frameworks
- Institutional capacity
- Social safety nets
- Governance mechanisms

History shows: The complementary investments determine whether technology delivers shared prosperity or concentrated gains.

---

### 2. Start Now, Start Small, But Start

**The cost of delay compounds**: Competitors gain experience, talent flows elsewhere, institutional capacity atrophies.

**But start smart**: Choose high-impact, lower-risk use cases. Build governance alongside deployment. Measure rigorously. Learn and iterate.

> "Waiting for certainty guarantees being left behind."
> 

---

### 3. Make This a Societal Transition, Not Just a Tech Upgrade

**The stakes are higher than efficiency gains:**

- Labor markets will be disrupted
- Income distribution will shift
- Power relationships will change

**Requirements for success:**

- Engage workers and unions early
- Address displacement proactively
- Design policies to share benefits
- Maintain social cohesion
- **Learn from globalization's failures**

**Remember**: The populism we see today stems from how poorly we managed globalization. We cannot afford to repeat that mistake with AI.

---

### 4. A Question for Collective Action

Individual institutional action matters. But this room represents something more powerful: **the regulatory architecture of Asia's financial system**.

**What can we do together that none of us can do alone?**

Possible areas for regional coordination:

- **Shared AI stress testing frameworks** for financial stability
- **Cross-border model validation standards** to reduce duplication
- **Regional compute infrastructure** (pool resources like SEA-LION)
- **Coordinated approach to AI governance** in financial regulation
- **Joint talent development programs** and knowledge exchange
- **Early warning system** for AI-driven market risks

**Challenge to this group**: Within 6 months, could we establish an Asia-Pacific Central Bank AI Working Group? Regular convenings, shared research, coordinated experiments.

The technology will arrive whether we coordinate or not. But coordinated responses amplify impact and reduce individual risk.
