**Will we manage AI's challenges better than we managed globalization, or repeat those mistakes?** Artificial intelligence is transforming our world faster than any previous technological revolution. Through live demonstrations and lessons from past disruptions, this interactive session explores the critical challenges facing central banks—both the unprecedented opportunities and the inevitable instability ahead. **Prompt**: "I'm a central bank governor. Generate three scenarios for 2026: baseline, downside shock, upside surprise. Cover GDP, inflation, unemployment, policy implications. Context: small open economy, trade-dependent, 3.2% inflation."

**Point**: "AI is your red team. It pattern-matches against every IMF report, BIS paper, Fed decision instantly."

# PART II: WHAT WE CAN DO (30 minutes)

## 4. Historical Lessons: Why the Transition Matters More Than the Technology (8 min)

### The Pattern from Past Disruptions

**Industrial Revolution (1760-1840)**: Engels' Pause

- Technology arrived fast; wage gains took 60+ years
- Required complementary investments: education, infrastructure, legal reform

**Electricity (1880s-1920s)**: The Factory Layout Problem

- Productivity gains only appeared when factories *redesigned* around electricity
- **Lesson**: Technology ≠ Transformation

**Information Revolution (1980s-2000s)**: The Solow Paradox

- "Computers everywhere except in productivity statistics"
- Took 20+ years; meanwhile: hollowed middle class, wage stagnation, populism

### The Central Question for This Room

> **"Will we manage the AI transition better than we managed globalization? Because the social and political costs of getting this wrong are already visible."**
> 

**Key Insight**: The transitions that worked best had **strong public sector leadership** coordinating investments in education, infrastructure, legal reform, and social safety nets.

# PART III: CHALLENGES AHEAD (25 minutes)

## 7. Five Critical Challenges for Central Banks (20 min)

### Challenge 1: Financial Stability Risks & The AI Bubble

**Carlota Perez's Framework: Understanding Technology Bubbles**

Perez's *Technological Revolutions and Financial Capital* (2002) shows every major technological revolution follows a pattern: **bubble → crash → golden age**

**The Historical Pattern:**
Every major revolution from railways (1840s) to electricity (1890s) to automobiles (1920s) to information technology (2000) followed this cycle. The bubble finances infrastructure build-out; the crash clears excess; the golden age delivers broad prosperity—**but only with proper institutional reform**.

**Perez's Two-Phase Pattern:**

*Phase 1: Installation (Financial Capital Dominates)*
- Speculation creates asset bubbles and finances infrastructure
- **Inevitable crash** when reality fails to meet expectations

*Phase 2: Deployment (Production Capital Takes Over)*
- Post-crisis regulation and reform enable broad adoption
- Productivity gains materialize across the economy
- "Golden Age" of shared prosperity—**if managed well**

**The Turning Point Requires Active Government** to ensure the crash doesn't kill productive deployment and that benefits are broadly shared.

**Where Are We Now?**

**Signs We're in Installation Phase:**

- $200B+ AI investment (2023-2024)
- NVIDIA market cap: $300B → $3T+ (2022-2024)
- AI startups at 50-100x revenue multiples
- $500B in data center investments announced
- "AI-washing" to boost stock prices

**What Makes the AI Bubble Different:**

1. **Speed**: Compressed to 2-3 years vs. decades for previous revolutions
2. **Concentration**: Few providers creates "too big to fail" dynamics
3. **Model Homogeneity**: Banks using similar AI models may act identically in crisis, amplifying boom-bust cycles
4. **Geopolitical Overlay**: US-China competition overrides normal market discipline

**Financial Stability Implications:**

**Pre-Crash (Now):**

- Asset price inflation in AI sectors
- Misallocation of capital at scale
- Banking system exposure to AI valuations
- Pension funds and sovereign wealth overexposed

**During Crash:**

- Contagion to broader equity markets
- Credit crunch as AI-focused banks face losses
- Flight to safety, liquidity stress

**Post-Crash (The Crucial Phase):**

- Financial stabilization (traditional crisis tools)
- **But also:** Ensure crash doesn't kill productive deployment
- Distinguish speculative excess from genuine innovation
- Launch regulatory reform to enable "golden age"

**The Railway Lesson:**

Railway mania (1840s) bankrupted investors but left Britain with essential infrastructure enabling a century of growth. **Key insight**: Bubbles can be financially destructive yet economically productive—if institutions manage the transition well.

**Central Bank Dilemma:**

- The bubble may be *necessary* to finance infrastructure build-out
- But bubbles always end badly for those caught
- Without proper management, society misses the golden age

**Three-Phase Policy Response for Central Banks:**

*Before the Crash:*
- Monitor AI asset valuations and stress test banking exposure
- Build international coordination on capital flows
- **Don't try to prevent the bubble** (historically impossible)—**do contain contagion channels**

*During the Crisis:*
- Deploy traditional crisis tools: liquidity provision, backstops, stability measures
- Protect critical AI infrastructure from fire-sale liquidation
- Distinguish genuine innovation from speculative excess

*Enabling the Golden Age:*
- Convene stakeholders to design regulatory frameworks for responsible deployment
- Support public investment in complementary infrastructure (education, safety nets, legal reform)
- Implement competition policy to prevent winner-take-all dynamics
- Design policies to ensure productivity gains are broadly shared

### Challenge 2: The Adoption Gap

**The 95% Problem**: Recent MIT research found that 95% of GenAI pilots fail to deliver value at scale

**Root Causes:**
- Lack of change management and cultural readiness
- Inadequate data infrastructure and quality
- Insufficient skills to deploy and maintain systems
- Unclear ROI metrics and success criteria

**Why Central Banks Are Especially Vulnerable**: Legacy systems, risk-averse culture, long procurement cycles, strict compliance requirements, and limited room for experimentation create additional barriers to adoption.

---

### Challenge 3: Labor Market Disruption

**What Makes This Different**: For the first time, AI automates *non-routine* cognitive work—research, analysis, even elements of policy analysis

**The Human-AI Division of Labor:**

- AI optimizes execution; humans prioritize goals
- AI recognizes patterns; humans challenge assumptions and innovate beyond them
- AI processes information; humans build trust and legitimacy
- AI follows instructions; humans exercise ethical judgment and write the rules

**Implications for Your Institutions**:
- Junior analyst roles face significant displacement
- Mid-career professionals need reskilling and role redefinition
- Senior judgment becomes more valuable than ever
- **The transition requires active workforce planning, not passive observation**

---

### Challenge 4: Geopolitics & Sovereignty

**Recent Developments:**

- US export controls on AI chips (H100, H200)
- Concentration of frontier AI in US + China
- Tier-2 countries face compute quotas

**Implications**: Dependence on foreign AI infrastructure, data sovereignty concerns, need for regional capabilities

**Regional Response Example**: SEA-LION provides an open-source alternative for Southeast Asian languages, reducing dependence on external providers while building local capacity.

---

### Challenge 5: Trust & Explainability

**Why This Matters Critically for Central Banks:**

- Decisions affect millions of citizens and require democratic legitimacy
- Public trust is essential for effectiveness
- "Black box" models undermine accountability and erode trust

**The Explainability Trilemma** (typically can achieve only 2 of 3):

1. **Accuracy**: Model performs well on real-world tasks
2. **Interpretability**: Humans understand how decisions are made
3. **Complexity**: Handles nuanced, multi-factor problems

**Managing the Trilemma**: Model cards, explainable AI techniques (SHAP, LIME), mandatory human-in-the-loop validation, comprehensive audit trails, and clear governance frameworks.

# CLOSING: The Choice Ahead (5 minutes)

## Return to Ricardo

In 1821, Ricardo believed machinery would help everyone. By 1823, he changed his mind—he saw workers devastated while prosperity concentrated at the top.

**But here's what's different now: We have two centuries of history to learn from. We know the patterns. We understand the risks. We can choose differently.**

---

## Three Imperatives for This Room

### 1. Invest in Adoption Architecture, Not Just Technology

The "boring" infrastructure matters most:

- Education and reskilling
- Legal and regulatory frameworks
- Institutional capacity
- Social safety nets
- Governance mechanisms

History shows: The complementary investments determine whether technology delivers shared prosperity or concentrated gains.

---

### 2. Start Now, Start Small, But Start

**The cost of delay compounds**: While you wait, competitors gain experience, talent flows elsewhere, and institutional capacity to adopt atrophies.

**But start smart**: Choose high-impact, lower-risk use cases. Build governance frameworks alongside deployment. Measure rigorously. Learn, iterate, and scale gradually.

> "Waiting for certainty guarantees being left behind. Perfect is the enemy of progress." 

---

### 3. Make This a Societal Transition, Not Just a Tech Upgrade

**The stakes extend far beyond efficiency gains:**

- Labor markets will be fundamentally disrupted
- Income distribution will shift, potentially dramatically
- Power relationships between capital and labor will change
- Social contracts will need renegotiation

**Requirements for successful transition:**

- Engage workers, unions, and civil society early in the process
- Address displacement proactively, not reactively
- Design policies explicitly to share productivity benefits broadly
- Invest in social safety nets and retraining programs
- Maintain social cohesion as the foundation for prosperity
- **Learn from how poorly we managed globalization**

**Critical reminder**: Much of the populism and social fragmentation we see today stems from how we managed—or failed to manage—globalization's dislocations. We cannot afford to repeat that mistake with AI.

## Important Notes on Technical Claims

**Statistics to Verify Before Delivery**:
- ChatGPT 100M users in 2 months: Verify current figures as this evolves rapidly
- DeepSeek 95% cost reduction: Confirm latest benchmarks
- MIT 95% GenAI pilot failure rate: Ensure you have the specific study citation

**Context-Dependent Claims**:
- The "AI bubble" thesis is analytical framework, not prediction. Present as one credible scenario among several
- Horizon timelines (2026, 2030) are estimates based on current trajectories, not certainties
- Labor displacement figures vary widely by study; present as ranges, not point estimates

**Presentation Integrity**: If questioned on a statistic you're uncertain about, acknowledge and offer to follow up. Credibility matters more than appearing to know everything.

## Speaker Notes

**Tone**: Conversational yet authoritative. Position yourself as a peer with relevant experience sharing lessons learned, not as a lecturer with all the answers.

**Style**: Use concrete examples from SEA-LION project, real-world deployments, and IMF/central bank experience. Ground abstract concepts in specific cases. Avoid jargon when possible.

**Pacing**: Watch for audience energy levels. Interactive elements are strategically placed to re-engage when attention typically flags. The Perez bubble section usually generates the most heated debate—lean into it and allow discussion.

**Adaptation**: Be ready to adjust depth based on audience signals. If financial stability resonates more than broader policy, go deeper there. If time runs short, compress Singapore section to 7 minutes and reduce one demo to 2 minutes. Buffer is built into Q&A time.

**Authenticity**: Acknowledge uncertainties honestly and openly. You don't have all the answers—neither does anyone else. That's precisely why we need cross-institutional collaboration and shared learning.
